Painted theatre backdrop, tall portrait (2:3). A storm-tossed sea that runs back into fog. Along the bottom third is a
churning field of SMALL, many, irregular breaking waves seen from a low viewpoint: crests curling and collapsing in
every direction, white foam boiling over dark water, spray and spindrift torn off the tops, foam lace and streaks
trailing between the crests. Some crests carry a Hokusai-like claw curl, but loose and broken, never repeated in rows
and never like a stencil or cut-out board. Waves are smallest and densest near the horizon and only modestly larger
at the bottom edge: no single giant wave, nothing bigger than a hand-span of the canvas. The rough water gradually calms
and dissolves into a vast deep, dark teal-grey ocean, then a thick sea fog bank, then a low, soft horizon about 35% up
from the bottom. Above it is a heavy overcast pearl-grey sky that darkens toward the top, with no sun, moon or stars.

Movement: diagonal energy, wind from the left, foam and spray flicked rightward, a sense of swell lifting and falling.
Palette: ink-dark teal and slate in the troughs, pewter mid-sea, bone, ivory and dirty-cream foam (matching cream stage
waves), faint cold silver on the horizon water. Muted, not saturated.
Style: hand-painted scenic artist's backdrop in matte distemper, loose expressive brushwork with dry-brush foam and
scumbled spray, classical atmospheric perspective, melancholic. Painterly, not photographic, not graphic or flat-vector.
Even frontal lighting, no vignette. No people, boats, birds, land, text or frame.
Avoid: neat repeating wave rows, outlined or scrolled cut-out shapes, a calm mirror sea in the foreground, giant
foreground waves, photoreal surf photography, vivid blue, sunsets, a hard horizon line.
