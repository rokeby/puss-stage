### The cats' feet follow the camera, not the floor

Two streams measured this and the second corrected the first. The **authored**
values:

- **P01**: CameraOffset.z 150, position offset −155.49 → cat at **−5.5 cm**, sunk.
- **P02–P06**: CameraOffset.z 180, offset −175.88 → **+4.1 cm**, hovering.
  (P03's Landscape sits ~+10, so its cat is ~6 cm into the ground in playback.)

The chain is: cat root z = rig z + position offset, and rig z = route z (0
everywhere) + CameraOffset.z + the Height channel. **So the cat's feet are pinned
to the camera's height, not to the floor.** That is the actual defect; the
numbers are symptoms.

**A −11.0 cm reading for P02 and P03 was stale, not authored.** Those two rig
actors are *saved in the level* at z=164.9 instead of 180 — evaluated state from
some earlier pass, persisted by a level save. That is precisely the effect
discovered a few hours earlier: **a level save carries evaluated-but-not-dirty
state, not just the caller's change.** The first measurement read the static
editor snapshot, which is honest data but not the playback state; the next
evaluation of either Passage restores 180. One stream's finding explained the
other's anomaly.

**And the Passages don't share an eye height.** P01 is shot at 150 cm and
everything else at 180. The cat discrepancy is a symptom of that. Whether P01
should be 30 cm lower than the rest of the show is yours.

**The fix is not to retune six offsets.** Matching today's camera heights would
re-break the moment anyone keys camera height, because the cat would rise and
fall with the shot. The durable form derives the cat's z from the **floor**, and
of the two ways to do that, one is now ruled out:

- **A downward trace will not work.** Every structure layer — boards and
  understage included — is NoCollision, so a cat tracing down would pass through
  the boards and hit nothing (or a curtain's catch box, or P03's Landscape, which
  does collide). It would need collision added to the ground layer first: C++, a
  build, and an unbudgeted cost.
- **Route z + the structure actor's `Ground.FloorHeightCm`** needs no collision.
  That value is already per-Passage, it *is* the height the boards are laid at,
  and it moves with the floor — so the cat can never disagree with the boards by
  construction. P03 needs its own value until its Landscape goes.

**One creative consequence, since the choice is not purely technical.** The
floor-height approach means the cat stands on *the floor*. The trace approach
would have meant the cat stands on *whatever is beneath it* — including your
fallen cloth piles. A cat that walks over a fallen curtain is a different animal
from one that walks through it. The cheap option is right for now, but it
quietly forecloses the more interesting one.

## The editor has been running at 2.5 seconds per frame, idle

Found by Soft goods while benching. Two causes:

1. **Background throttling.** `bThrottleCPUWhenNotForeground` is unset in both the
   project config and your per-user settings, so the engine default applies:
   throttle when the editor is not the foreground window. **We have deliberately
   kept it behind other windows all night**, because that is what the offscreen
   capture route was built for. So every script and capture has run through an
   editor throttling itself because nobody was looking at it.
2. **400 HTTP timeouts** to `google.com/generate_204`, 3 s each, from 01:55:27
   onward. I first reported these as a *second, independent* cause. The timing
   says otherwise, and I think **they are a symptom of (1), not a cause**:

   - They fire **one per frame** — 395 distinct frame numbers across 400 timeouts.
   - The **median gap between them is exactly 5.0 s**, which is two of Soft goods'
     measured 2.48 s tick intervals.

   HTTP completions are processed on the game thread tick. With a 3 s timeout and
   ticks 2.5 s apart, a request is checked at +2.5 s (alive) and again at +5.0 s
   (expired) — so it times out *regardless of the network*, which is why a shell
   reaches the same URL in 57 ms. The editor cannot process the reply in time
   because it is barely ticking.

   That is an inference from timing, not proof — I could not trace the caller,
   because the URL is hardcoded in C++ and this is an installed engine with no
   sources. But it fits exactly, and it means **the fix is probably one setting,
   not two**.

**Nothing is changed.** This affects your machine with two editors open, and I am

### One interaction to know about before you turn a blend up

The Go1 receiver re-centres the dog on **view-target change**, and during a blend
the engine still reports the *outgoing* camera. So with a blend above zero the dog
steers the outgoing camera for the whole blend, then re-centres again when the
blend completes — and if its stick is deflected at that moment the view **steps**,
by up to 30.3° in Gaze mode. At a cut that step is hidden by the cut; after a blend
it lands mid-motion and is visible.

Look & camera has a fix (preferring the pending view target while a blend is in
flight, so the dog steers the incoming shot from the first frame). It is a no-op
at blend 0, so today stays byte-identical. **Approved, pending a build.**

**Your call, separately:** should a re-centre subtract the dog's current stick
offset, so a deflected stick never steps the view *even at a cut*? That changes
today's behaviour. There is a real argument against: in Gaze mode the dog's look is
meant to be **absolute**, and you may want the step at a cut precisely because it is
honest about where the animal is actually looking. That is a question about what
the dog *is* in this piece, not an engineering one.
