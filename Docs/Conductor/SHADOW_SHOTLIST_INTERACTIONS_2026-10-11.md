# Shadow shot list for the interaction sequences (for the Gobo task)

**Sources:**
- the script `PUSS_script_25-07-26 — Organised Review Version` (Drive);
- Cameron's `PUSS RUNNING ORDER` (Drive, 1 Oct);
- the project's interaction sequences, `Content/.../LS_Interaction_0N_*`.

**Pipeline:** `Docs/ShadowGoboPipeline_2026-10-06.md`. Film hand or puppet shadows on a sheet with an iPhone (1080p30, HDR off). Then `Tools/ShadowGobo/make_gobo_flipbook.py` builds a flipbook light function, and the frame is keyed by an MPC scalar.

**Cast:** two silhouettes throughout: **PUSS** (the cat) and **THE BOY**. In each interaction one object arrives (the dog "offloads" it, in the running order): turnip, clam, scroll, boots, abacus.

**Not yet measured:** the duration of each `LS_Interaction_0N` sequence. Time each clip to its sequence length, and leave a 1–2 s plate at each end so the curtain/shadow hand-over has a still frame.

**How the beats read:** each beat below is one shot or flipbook. Beats 1–4 tell the story; the closing beat is the recurring refrain.

---

## I1, Washing the Boy (`LS_Interaction_01_WashingTheBoy`), after Story 1, Animals by the Lake
The script says: "Here there will be a shadow of the boy in the game engine and that of a cat."
1. The boy sits hunched (dirty, rejected).
2. Puss arrives and drains the small fountain: a water shape tipping or pouring. This is the "Water drained LIVE+BACKING" cue.
3. Puss licks or washes the boy: the cat's head bobs repeatedly against the boy's head and shoulders.
4. The boy straightens ("shines like alabaster"). He is still wanting.
5. **The boy whimpers:** a small shudder or head drop.

The stage note says the light turns red here. If that holds, the gobo frames read as black shapes on red.

## I2, Turnips (`LS_Interaction_02_Turnips`), after Story 2, The Shrine
1. Puss enters carrying the turnips. The prop is a **turnip**.
2. Puss presents them, and the boy grabs them.
3. The boy eats them all quickly, in fast chomping repeats, and the turnip silhouettes shrink away.
4. Stillness: the boy is empty again and sad, thinking of his dead father. Slow head down.
5. **The boy whimpers.** Optionally, "Dog happy move" (running order 22).

## I3, Forgets His Name (`LS_Interaction_03_ForgetsHisName`), after Story 3, The Song of Forgetting
1. Puss sits beside the boy and sings (live with pedal?, running order 29). The cat's head is raised and its mouth opens rhythmically. The prop is a **clam**, offloaded.
2. The boy sways, soothed, and his head lolls.
3. Forgetting: the boy's outline goes slack. His tongue "lolls like a wet tennis ball": a floppy tongue shape.
4. The boy coughs, in sharp jerks.
5. **The boy gives a big snivelling whimper** (running order 31: the biggest whimper of the show).

## I4, Gets a Name (`LS_Interaction_04_GetsAName`), after Story 4, The School / Cagliuso
1. Puss carries a torn page in his mouth. The prop is a **scroll** (the page with "Cagliuso").
2. Puss gives the page to the boy, and the boy holds it up.
3. The boy turns the page over and looks at it: "what's in a name?"
4. A pause; the boy lowers the page.
5. **The boy whimpers.**

## I5, Becomes King (`LS_Interaction_05_BecomesKing`), after Story 5, Mountains: the "Puss-boy curtain scene"
1. A pair of **boots** pops in (squeezed out from between the mountains). The prop is the **boots**.
2. Puss admires them ("This is a fine pair of boots") and holds them up.
3. Puss pulls the boots on, one leg and then the other, with a comic struggle.
4. Puss struts and shows them to the boy: "Now you are king and I have boots!"
5. **The boy whimpers mournfully:** a slow, drawn-out sag.

## Also shadow work, outside the five interactions
- **The opening** (running order 2): the old man and his three sons. He hands a strainer to one, a ruler to another, and to the third, a cat. The old man closes his eyes, the boy whimpers, and the cat purrs as the boy pets its back.
- **The ending** (running order 48): after the Void, a sixth handover. The boy receives an **abacus** and counts "1, 2, 3, 4, 5…" while Puss slowly falls asleep. Then the shadows of stage workers sweep the stage floor and turn the lights off (Lights out, 49).

## For the Gobo task: the cheapest way to film it
- **One take per beat:** about 3–8 s each at 8 fps in the flipbook. A whole interaction is then 5 short takes stitched by the MPC key, so each beat can be retimed in rehearsal without re-filming.
- **The whimper is reusable:** it's the same action in every interaction, so one good whimper take can be used in all five. Film a small, a big and a mournful version.
- **Shared props:** each prop is a cut-out (turnip ×3, clam, a torn page, boots, abacus).
- **Shared bodies:** Puss and the boy are the same two puppets throughout.
