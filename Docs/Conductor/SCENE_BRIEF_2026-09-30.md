# What each Scene is — Gary's brief, 30 September 2026

From a voice note. **The first description of what the six Scenes actually
contain**, given in terms of scale and light, and how each sits in a hangar.

---

## The six

**SCENE 1 — animals in conversation.** Human scale, theatrical scale, **small**.
A backdrop with an **overturned train** and other assets.

**SCENE 2 — a shrine.** **Architecturally higher**, but essentially the same
thing: a **diorama** in a **relatively dark, low-lit** scene.

**SCENE 3 — the shore people.** Swaying gently by the shore with their clam.
**Broad daylight** — *"a kind of theatrical cold outdoor light"*. **New backdrops
needed.** Still the same diorama scale.

**SCENE 4 — a cathedral-like space.** **Wings used as architectural panels.**
Children and a **large pigeon**. Human scale, but a **cooler, architectural** lit
space, with **artificial light through artificial windows within the stage**.

**SCENE 5 — the village and the mountains.** Where it *"gets dramatically
different"*. We arrive on a **village in a mountain valley** — a larger human-ish
scale, the village with its people and animals. **Then we zoom OUT**, not to
realistic scale but far enough to see **the two mountains either side as
CHARACTERS**, having a conversation — **and eventually they come together and
crush the village.**

> *"That does have a kind of dramatic scalar effect that starts within the
> hangar box, but needs to exceed it somehow, and needs to somehow open out from
> the box."*

**SCENE 6 — an old lady in a cosmic void.** *"Works perfectly well in a kind of
blacked out space"*, and **possibly with some of the stage pieces having fallen
down.**

## What this settles, and what it changes

### Five of six are dioramas. One is not.

**Scenes 1, 2, 3, 4 and 6 are human/theatrical scale** and sit inside a hangar as
built. **Scene 5 is the exception, deliberately** — it starts in the box and must
**exceed** it.

**That reframes today's Scene 5 measurement.** Its hangar was built at
200 × 150 × 50 m and measured as effectively **unseen** from the cat: black until
about 12 s, and only **2.8% of the frame lit** after the uplights against Scene
4's 20.7%, because a rig that scales in number still has its lights 100–150 m
from anything visible. **The conclusion — "a room that big needs a different
lighting approach, not a proportionally larger one" — stands and now has a
reason: the room is not supposed to be a room.** Scene 5's problem was never
lighting a hangar. It is that Scene 5 breaks the hangar.

### Scene 5's scale-break and the SCENE EXIT may be one mechanism

The exit proposal has the hangar's **far wall opening** — a portal-sized panel
falling outward as a drawbridge. **Scene 5 needs the box to open out.** Those may
be the same move used twice: once to leave, once to let a scene exceed its room.
**Worth holding as a possibility, not a plan** — nobody has designed either yet.

### Scene 3 is the only DAYLIGHT scene, and it lost its light today

*"Broad daylight, a kind of theatrical cold outdoor light."* **Every other scene
is dark, low-lit, cool-architectural or blacked out**, and the whole arrival
grammar — *"virtually unlit, lit by one exit-sign lamp, then the lights ping on"*
— is built for darkness. **Scene 3 is the one that does not fit it.**

And today its floor changed: the wet ground was catching the scene's light as a
bright pool, oak absorbs it, **the floor band dropped 78%.** That was already on
his sheet as a look question. **In a broad-daylight scene it matters more, not
less** — and P03 is the site he named as where he will first test characters with
a hangar.

### Scene 4's light comes from the STAGE, not from outside

*"Artificial light coming through artificial windows within the stage."* So its
cathedral light is **rig lighting pretending to be windows** — which is the same
grammar as the lattice reading as structure, and the same argument for **not**
dropping the exposure away from the rig's own lamps.

### Scene 6 wants what its hangar already is

**Blacked out, and possibly with stage pieces fallen.** Its hangar is the roofless
one, and its empty roof beat has been an open question all day. **A void with
collapsed structure is closer to "the roof never arrives" than to a fault.**
