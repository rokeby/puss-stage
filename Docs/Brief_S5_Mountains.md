## Where this sits

Puss, a robot dog, walks a picaresque through a mechanical theatre. The show
alternates **Passages** — travelling shots down a corridor of moving painted
wings, an "infinity stage" — with **Scenes**, fixed set pieces at the end of each
corridor. Between them, a curtain closes and the boy and the cat talk in shadow.

Scene 5 is the fifth of six, reached through Passage 5. It is followed by the
boy becoming King, then a final passage into the Void.

**The dramatic job:** the mountains are the last thing larger than the boy. After
this he is crowned, and then there is nothing. So the scene should feel like being
in the presence of something that does not care, and does not hurry.

## The idea

**Mountains speak to each other in rumbles, and the rumbles are subtitled.**

They are not characters with faces. They are masses with opinions, exchanged over
geological time and rendered as surtitles at the top of the frame. The comedy and
the melancholy both come from the gap between the scale of the speaker and the
banality of what is said.

The audience reads the mountains' conversation while a small robot dog stands in
front of the screen, physically present, not understanding it.

## Duration and structure — locked to the audio

`Mountain_Scene.wav`, **152.341 seconds** (2:32). Analysis is in

The music's own shape, from the analysis — build to this, not against it:

| Time | Character | Note |

| 0:00 – 1:14 | quiet, slowly falling | **Nearly half the piece before anything happens.** Hold. Resist cutting. |
| 1:14 – 1:40 | quiet, drifting up | first stirring |
| 1:40 – 2:01 | **building** | the only real build in the piece |
| 2:01 – 2:09 | releasing | |
| 2:09 – 2:22 | quiet | |
| 2:22 – 2:27 | **sharp release** | steepest move in the scene — something ends here |
| 2:27 – 2:32 | quiet | tail into the curtain |

The single most important note: **the first 65% of this piece is quiet.** The
temptation will be to fill it. Don't. The scene earns its one build by having
waited for it.

## Surtitles

- Delivered as text keyed inside the sequence — see the contract.
- Style: clean font, black bar, top of frame. One shared widget; don't restyle.
- **Timing is the joke.** A rumble that takes eleven seconds to say four words is
  the point. Let lines sit far longer than reading speed requires.
- Text will be written by Gary — send back proposed *timings and line count*, and
  the words get fitted to them. Roughly 6–12 exchanges across the piece.

## Camera

- Camera owner for this Scene: **Timeline** — author the camera normally.
- Entry frame matches the end of Passage 5; handback shot at the end matches the
  start of Passage 6. Both are generated by the handoff tool; leave them alone.
- The frame is a **3.5 m wide screen at 3 m viewing distance**, behind a live
  performer and a robot dog. Compose for that: readable at a glance, no critical
  detail at the edges, nothing that depends on being scrutinised.

## Look

- The show's visual world is painted theatrical flats, LiDAR-scanned architecture
  and a deliberately artificial stage machine. **The mountains should not be
  photoreal terrain.** They are scenery — closer to a painted backdrop with
  parallax than to a landscape render.
- Reference the existing wing families for the register, particularly the
  Passage 5 bird-to-mountain set, which this scene is the destination of.
- Lighting: no sunset, no day cycle. The show has removed its celestial default
  deliberately — the stage is lit, not the world. Stage lighting states will be
  designed against yours, so tell us what you want rather than assuming ambient.
